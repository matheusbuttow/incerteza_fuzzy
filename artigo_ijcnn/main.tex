\begin{abstract}
Deep classifiers often fail with high confidence, which is critical in medical applications such as skin-cancer screening.
We propose an interpretable uncertainty detector that reads the network's internal behavior---linear probes at each block,
agreement across augmented views, and disagreement among nearest neighbors in feature space---and combines these signals
with an additive fuzzy model: a zero-order Takagi--Sugeno system with single-antecedent rules and logistic output.
Each rule has a readable form (``IF final-layer agreement is LOW THEN risk of error increases'') and a learned weight.
On HAM10000 (benign vs.\ malignant, EfficientNet-B0) and seven additional scenarios (CIFAR-10 with and without label noise,
CIFAR-100, SVHN, MNIST), the method matches or exceeds the best baseline in all scenarios
(AUROC 0.916 on HAM10000 and 0.903 on CIFAR-10 with 30\% label noise), is significantly better than Chen et al.\ in
18 of 26 runs and never significantly worse, and explains each decision through its rules.

\subsection{Additive fuzzy model}
Each feature $x_j$ is covered by three triangular terms (LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH) centred at its 5th, 50th and 95th percentiles;
for the network output $P$ in the binary case the centres are fixed at $\{0, \tau, 1\}$, where $\tau$ is the decision threshold.
The risk of error is
\begin{equation}
  r(\mathbf{x}) = \sigma\Big(b + \sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{3} w_{jk}\,\mu_{jk}(x_j)\Big),
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{jk}$ is the membership of $x_j$ in term $k$ and $w_{jk}$ is the weight of rule
``IF $x_j$ is $k$ THEN risk changes by $w_{jk}$''. Weights are learned by logistic regression.
